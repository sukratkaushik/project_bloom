## Strict do-not rules

The following are **all banned**, in order of severity:

1. **Never recreate the logo as SVG paths, hand-drawn vectors, or any flat geometric reconstruction.** The painted lotus has soft gradient blends that don't translate to flat vector. Any redraw will be wrong.
2. **Never replace the logo with an illustrative substitute** (a different lotus, a different flower, a tulip, etc.).
3. **Never change the colors** of the lotus — pink upper petals, saffron lower petals, mint leaf accents, white stem highlight. They are baked into the PNG.
4. **Never stretch, squash, skew, or rotate** the logo. Always preserve aspect ratio (it's a 1:1 square).
5. **Never add a drop shadow, glow, or outline** to the lotus itself. The lotus sits flat on Sandalwood.
6. **Never place the colored logo on busy photographic backgrounds.** Either use Sandalwood, a brand-tinted card, or sit the photo behind a Sandalwood card and put the lotus on the card.
7. **Never put the logo on pure white `#FFFFFF` full-bleed.** Use Sandalwood `#FDFBF7`.
8. **Never recolor the logo** to be a single color — there is no "monochrome variant." If a single-color rendering is needed (print, deboss, foil), commission a proper monochrome version from a designer; do not invent one.
9. **Never crop the logo.** It includes mint leaves at the base — those are part of the mark.

## Clear space

Minimum padding around the logo = the width of one petal (~12% of the bounding box). This is approximately the inset used in all derived lockups.

| Surface | Minimum size of the lotus |
|---|---|
| Favicon (browser tab) | 16 × 16 px — at this size the lotus reads as a soft warm blob; that's OK |
| App icon (iOS / Android) | Render from `logo.png` at 1024 × 1024 source |
| Marketing header | 32 px tall |
| In-app header | 24 px tall (matches the shipped dashboard) |
| Print | 12 mm (0.5 in) tall |
| Deck title slide | 200 px tall |

## Backgrounds — what works

| Background | Logo behavior |
|---|---|
| Sandalwood `#FDFBF7` | ✅ Default. Best. |
| White `#FFFFFF` | ⚠️ Works at small sizes, but Sandalwood is correct everywhere. |
| Sage `#8AB6A3` (button) | ❌ The colored logo doesn't read well on saturated Sage. Use a white Sandalwood card on top of the Sage and put the logo on the card. |
| Charcoal `#2C3E50` (dark mode) | ⚠️ The pink and saffron read OK against Charcoal, but the white stem highlight competes. Test before shipping. |
| Photography | ❌ Never directly on a photo. Always on a Sandalwood card on top of the photo. |

## Regenerating derived assets

If you ever need to re-render the lockups (for example, you've replaced `logo.png` with an updated version):

```bash
cd ~/gitclones/Project_Bloom_Design
node build-logo-assets.mjs
```

This regenerates every lockup PNG from the current `logo.png`. Commit the new PNGs.

---

## Why no SVG version

The lotus has soft watercolor gradient blending across the petals. Reproducing that in flat SVG either (a) loses the softness and looks like a children's-book sticker, or (b) requires hundreds of gradient stops which is fragile and inconsistent across renderers.

Until someone produces an authored vector source from the original artist, **the PNG is the canonical form**. Period.

---

## When the logo will get an SVG / authored vector

We need one of:
1. The original Illustrator / Affinity / Figma source file from the designer who created the lotus.
2. A clean, gradient-faithful SVG redraw produced by a designer (not by an LLM or an auto-vectorizer).
3. A new version of the brand mark commissioned with vector formats as a deliverable from day one.

Until then: **`logo.png` is the only canonical logo. Everything is derived from it.**
